# Child appearance and automatic review recovery — 18 October 2026

The pilot exposed children who looked older or less like their photograph, repeated the reference portrait's expression and head turn, or lacked required winter hats. Three synchronous continuity questions also timed out without receipts and exhausted the historical automatic continuation limit. This change adds an explicit per-world subject contract and durable background continuity, so the engine can reject these appearances, repaint them and continue without discarding uncertain charges.

Branch `codex/subject-recovery-20261018`, base `4f680644`. Implementation began with Claude Max; Codex owns the integrated changes and verification. This document describes source behavior. It does **not** assert that new live art, a complete game, a lower average price or a deployment has been verified.

## Scope and authorization

Enrollment is opt-in and immutable: `local-patch-subject-upgrade/v1` records the owner, child age, exact original-photo and canonical-identity hashes, all authored subject plans, review release, continuity transport and recovery policy. Games without enrollment retain their historical questions, pins and paid receipts. No migration, global model switch, new game, new identity purchase or payment rewrite is required.

The current QA pilot has a total supplier cap of USD6. The prior evidence records USD2.348769 settled and USD0.198 retained for three uncertain questions, a USD2.546769 commitment. These are usage-based estimates and reservations, not verified provider invoices. Remaining room is USD3.453231; neither the upgrade nor unused room authorizes an increase. All 27 playable hides and the existing visual, accounting, privacy and deletion gates remain mandatory.

## Appearance and painting

- `subject-policy.ts` assigns each authored hide a contextual action, head direction, gaze and expression. Three hides on one board receive distinct head directions, expressions and hand/body gestures. Authored feet, cover and support remain authoritative. Antarctica explicitly requires a warm hat without hiding the recognisable face.
- The subject reader checks original-photo likeness, agreement with the canonical illustrated face, face visibility, apparent age, planned action/head/expression and required clothing. Every check requires an observation; a failure needs a location. An uncertain or contradictory answer never passes. A broad apparent-age range is uncertainty; an estimate clearly outside the stated child's age derives a failure even if the model claims pass. Subject uncertainty also blocks the historical peeking age-only waiver.
- Enrolled new paintings receive the scene crop, native canonical portrait and privately owned original photograph, bounded to 384px. The photograph supplies identity and age only; the scene supplies style, light and geometry. Ownership, private status, liveness, age and source bytes are verified before use.
- A world pinned to exact LOW can keep LOW for pose or wardrobe corrections. A face/likeness/age fault, final attempt, diagnosed redraw, or unrecognised base policy selects explicit MEDIUM. This choice and the source-photo/direction hashes belong to the new render fingerprint. Interrupted attempts resolve their attempt number before constructing the direction. Historical purchased keys replay their original fingerprint; a different quality never silently replaces a purchased operation.

## Two independent judges, with subject checks inside Opus

For new candidate pixels, the complete Opus HIGH quality inspection and subject inspection use one Anthropic Batch request when the exact maximum quote fits the existing quality reservation. An oversized packet keeps the complete ordinary quality inspection and a separate subject inspection. This choice is deterministic before purchase; evidence, output caps and reservations are unchanged. The versioned `quality-subject-answer/v1` wrapper requires both complete inner documents. The strict reader does not extract a convenient fragment from surrounding prose. Raw paid provider text is preserved; `qualityStageRaw` and `subjectStageRaw` derive the two readings deterministically.

In a combined request, a subject failure or uncertainty rejects and routes the candidate to repair before purchasing Sol continuity. Sol HIGH remains an independent continuity judge. A verified legacy quality answer can replay for free and use a separate subject-only Opus Batch question. A fresh continuity pass is checkpointed before a separate subject question and reused only for identical image, geometry, identity, question and settled bill. The separate path can cost an additional Opus inspection; the normal budget gate admits it individually within the same world cap.

Every publication/protection read requires all three logical proofs: quality, continuity and subject. A combined subject proof must share the exact Opus request key, fingerprint, raw body and charge with its quality proof, and match the enrolled photograph and hide plan. The report counts this shared charge once. The ledger remains the accounting authority. A subject-format retry uses a new bounded evidence attempt rather than replaying the same unreadable wrapper forever.

## Durable continuity and uncertain charges

New enrolled Sol continuity uses the same HIGH model in OpenAI Responses background mode, `store:false`, with a new transport/policy fingerprint. The provider response ID is durably recorded. A round has one create claim; subsequent slices poll that ID. Failed GETs wait. Missing, expired or unpriced results remain held at the full unknown reservation. A documented refusal before creation is recorded from real evidence; it does not trigger an automatic paid fallback.

`advanceContinuityInBackground` reconciles existing recorded responses at the start of a slice, before held-job or hide-selection gates. It validates the current world's intent and matching ledger reservation and only polls recorded IDs or records a genuinely lost create. It never creates or reserves a new question. This prevents an accepted sibling or another held question from delaying an already-created response until temporary retention expires.

Historical `world-automatic-review-recovery/v1` retains its limit of two. An enrolled world may use version 2 for at most four additional admitted interruptions; a key already continued is never continued twice. The retained original unknown bill is not released or fabricated. Only the exact no-response review shape with genuine ledger and retained-byte bindings is eligible. Background results containing an unpriced answer remain a safe hold rather than qualifying as no-response recovery.

Planning and recovery select a specific hide with `onlyHideId`. They do not rely on the board's ordinary next-candidate picker: enrollment deliberately removes old subject-less protection, and Batch may have left an interrupted GENERATED appearance without a quality checkpoint while a sibling has an old acceptance. Only uncontinued unknown keys are matched; old paid questions remain candidates in their original form.

## Subject painter correction after actual art review

`local-patch-subject-paint/v2` is a distinct painter direction for new purchases in already enrolled worlds. The historical v19 support recipe asks for relaxed arms, while the subject plan requires another gesture; the historical diagnosis also asks to preserve the faulty pose. The new recipe keeps authored support, depth and safe geometry and lets the immutable subject plan alone specify the arms, head, gaze and expression. A planned-action repair now repeats the actual action rather than only its check code. The enrolled subject policy, plan digest, strict acceptance reader, age checks, hat requirement and full-27 publication gate are unchanged.

Started image requests are matched against their exact current, v1-subject or pre-enrollment fingerprints. A new recipe is never substituted under a started key, a foreign fingerprint still refuses, and an unknown without a retained receipt remains unknown. New attempts retain distinct request identities.

`subject-format-recovery/v2` is a recovery diagnostic, not an acceptance-reader replacement. It checks every strict-schema issue in a retained subject answer. Only otherwise complete answers whose sole errors are observation/location strings over the existing 400-character maximum select the existing `compact/v1` question. The raw answer, original fault and previous history are retained unchanged. A newly overlong reply records the length category, so the existing compact escalation limit and total evidence-round cap still retire repeated failures. The same models, evidence, output caps and reservations remain in force.

This corrects a cause of repeated paid refusals; it does not establish successful art, a complete live world or a lower average price. Real generation must still pass the exact-commit release checks and the unchanged engine gates.

## Owner clarification of 18 October: subject v2, diagnosis context, progress, spend pause

Branch `claude/subject-policy-20261018`, base `247a12c6`. The owner's instruction: a child standing naturally with relaxed arms is a valid appearance; partial repetition of a pose is allowed and variety is judged across the collection. The true blockers stay: likeness to the source photograph, the stated age, a readable face, anatomy, integration, intact neighbours, the seam and the warm hat in Antarctica. The pilot's two New York refusals (`first-correction-rejected`, `self-repair-rejected`) failed v1 only on `plannedAction` (arms at the sides instead of a planned forearm); every check of who the child is had passed.

**`local-patch-subject/v2` (`subject-read/v2`), new questions only.** Five checks decide: `photoLikeness`, `canonicalAgreement`, `faceVisible`, `perceivedAge`, `requiredWardrobe`, read with v1's derivations unchanged (a fail must be located, a contradicted pass or a broad age range is unsure, an estimate outside the stated age fails, a required hat answered not-required is unsure, unsure never passes). The plan is offered as a suggestion; a fault about pose, head, gaze or expression is kept and decides nothing, while any other unclassified fault still blocks. Each answer also describes the appearance (head, expression, engagement, `portraitCopy`). Variety belongs to the whole collection (`subjectCollectionRefusal`), never to one board. A look is the pasted portrait itself (its head angle and smile, facing the viewer, doing nothing in the scene) or a head turn, expression and engagement together; children who share part of a look (the same smile, the same head turn) have different looks. Any look may repeat, the pasted portrait included, on one board or across boards. Only a material repetition is corrected: an appearance whose exact look is already shown by a third of the world (9 of 27 accepted appearances) is repainted with the suggestion and a variety repair. There is no per-board limit and no limit on any single feature. A v1 answer is read by v1 for good: v1 passes stand, and nothing is relabelled.

**Painter `local-patch-subject-paint/v3`.** The requirement is fixed wording: the photograph's face, the canonical hair kept exactly (length, fringe, layers; never cropped or tidied), the stated age, the hat where required. The plan is one natural option. A repair names its codes and quotes the defects the reviewer located on the refused painting (printable, at most four, 300 characters each). Started purchases replay their exact v2 or v1 direction; LOW stays the base painter and only a face, likeness or age fault, a final attempt or a diagnosed redraw selects MEDIUM.

**`subject-rejudge/v1`.** A FAILED appearance whose v1 subject stage refused it for the pose plan alone, whose own quality answer is acceptable, with no paid repair phase in flight, is asked the v2 question once about the SAME pixels. "Pose plan alone" is decided from the complete v1 answer, re-read by the v1 reader at the enrolled age (`poseOnlyV1Refusal`): every check of who the child is, their age, face and hat passed, at least one pose check did not, every located fault names a pose check, and no unclassified fault (a hair defect, say) was reported; the stored failed checks must agree. Its paid quality and continuity answers are reused under their own keys; it is never repainted for that refusal. The previous review, with its v1 stage and fingerprints, is appended to `reviewHistory` with `supersededBy: "subject-rejudge/v1"`; only the new v2 answer, bound to these pixels, can accept them. A refusal that also named the face, the age or the hat is repainted.

**`subject-diagnosis/v1`.** In an enrolled world each self-repair cycle freezes what the last review refused about the child: the v2 codes, the located defects, whether the defect is inside the painted child (`intrinsic`: likeness, age, face, hat, or the quality judge's own face or age doubt) and the source painting the refused composition came from. The diagnosis now also receives the original photograph (384px, last image, labelled as authority for likeness and age only). A plan that recomposes the very painting whose child was refused is refused before anything is bought; a different retained RAW or a redraw may still be chosen. Cycles frozen before this release keep their exact question.

**The switchover.** A v1 question already bought (or in a Batch round) about the same pixels and evidence round, whose answer was never recorded on the row, is resumed under v1 and never bought again in its v2 form, both as a bundle and as a separate subject question. Its answer is read under v1 exactly as when it was bought: a pass stands, an unreadable answer takes the evidence route, a refusal is a refusal. Only a refusal for the pose plan alone goes on to the v2 question, once; the v1 answer is kept beside the proof (`supersededSubject`), and publication requires any v1 answer a v2 answer replaced to be such a pose-only refusal.

**The refused source.** The guard's source is bound to the refused composition itself (`refusedCompositionSource`): a refused self-repair candidate records its own source (`compositionSource`), the binding frozen with a cycle survives a rejected plan, and an ordinary composition names its authenticated `renderPurchase`. A candidate written before the binding cannot say, and then no retained painting may be recomposed.

**`local-patch-progress/v1`.** In an enrolled world one ordered list serves repairs and paintings: paid work in flight first (a frozen diagnosis or plan), then the appearance with the fewest paid tries, then board order. The historical order ran every started repair before any second attempt; with about one paid step per slice, five appearances took a day of purchases while twenty-two waited. A repair stops after `SUBJECT_V2_REPAIR_CYCLES = 3` cycles started under v2. An appearance is at rest only when nothing is owed on its last picture (`localPatchRestOf`: an evidence question still to ask, a re-judgment or a player review keeps it working whatever its repair count). When every remaining appearance is at rest (spent repair cycles, no evidence question left, or any mixture of the two), the world parks at `local-patch:recovery-exhausted` naming each with its reason (`localPatchRestDecision`); an outage still being waited out waits instead. It is never READY without all 27.

**`local-patch-spend-pause/v1`.** On a QA deployment that does not permit the world's owner to spend, the queue no longer claims the world every minute to fail at its first paid step (the pilot's claim counter went from 229 to 240). The job is parked unclaimed at `local-patch:spend-paused` (fenced Game then Job; never over a live lease; never a job parked for a person) and the cron looks again every ten minutes. A deployment that lists the owner resumes it; reservations stay counted meanwhile.

**`local-patch:qa-pilot-completion/v1`.** The owner approved a larger budget if needed to complete the pilot world and named no figure. A working frame of USD12 cumulative, history and unknown reservations included, applies to `game_j9mpft6aemfij36zegjk:board-wizard` only, and only on top of its verified six-dollar pin (refund, deletion or ownership change removes it). Every other world, game and environment keeps its cap. Beyond the frame the engine stops; the calculation goes back to the owner.

## Slice pacing after the first live hour (18 October, evening)

On QA the release above ran every slice into the platform's 300-second limit (`Vercel Runtime Timeout Error`, after the tick's own `deadline-exceeded` at 270 s). A killed slice keeps its claim for the six-minute lease, so the world advanced one step every eleven minutes. The database shows why: after a review, a slice went on to the next board's review, and every such review first rebuilds that board's evidence, which took one to three minutes there; once a question no longer fitted its dispatch window nothing was bought, the review cost nothing, and the loop, which stops only after a paid review, went on to the next board until the platform stopped it. Nothing paid was lost: purchases check their own window.

**`local-patch-slice-pacing/v1`.** A board review, a free decision or the review of a complete board is started only while the slice has room for the longest seen on QA (`LOCAL_PATCH_BOARD_REVIEW_MIN_MS`, 180 s) and for twice the longest it has already done (`localPatchReviewFits`); otherwise the claim is handed back with the rest still runnable, and the next tick, a minute later, takes it. A board whose every appearance with review work has its question already sent, polled by this slice and still running at the provider (a Message Batches round or a background response) is not rebuilt meanwhile (`localPatchBoardAwaitsProvider`); it counts as a pending review, so the world neither finishes nor rests while it waits, and the slice's time goes to work that can move. A question is named only when the provider has just said it still runs: the batch pass's status read (`in_progress`) or the background pass's read (`queued`, `in_progress`) returns that explicitly (`running` on the gate), and `advanceQualityBatches` and `advanceContinuityInBackground` collect only those. A round the pass had no room to read, a read that failed, an ended batch whose results are not yet read, or a round still to be created, found or closed keeps its board in review, whose own path advances it (Codex, review of `75289afa`: a state read afterwards is not an observation). Both passes keep the slice's whole deadline for open work, as before, so later responses and answers are still reached behind slow failures; the batch pass takes open rounds before finished ones, and only finished rounds' housekeeping stops with room for one review (`LOCAL_PATCH_BOARD_REVIEW_MIN_MS` plus one phase margin before the deadline), so housekeeping that keeps failing slowly (Codex, review of `a7fc6067`) never leaves a settled answer undecided. At today's 270-second slices the floor is what decides (after a review of L seconds, 270 − L ≥ 180 already implies 2L ≤ 180); twice the longest review applies only to longer slices. Every review logs its board, kind, state and duration (`[local-patch] board review`; no child data), so the next slow step is visible instead of inferred.

Tests: the rule and its boundaries (`local-patch-progress.test.ts`); a whole v12 world in which one board's evidence takes 200 s of the clock: the slice starts no second review, hands its claim back before its deadline and the next slices take the board left over (`local-patch-dual-world.test.ts`); a board whose three questions wait in created batches is not rebuilt until the batches end, then decided; deletions that take 30 s each and fail slice after slice still leave room to decide a settled answer; a later answer is settled behind three reads that keep failing slowly, whatever the housekeeping deadline; only a batch the provider has just said runs is named, never one whose create may still answer, one not read, one whose read failed, or one that ended (`quality-batch.test.ts`, and for background responses `continuity-background.test.ts`); a slow free decision is not followed by another the slice cannot finish (`local-patch-dual-world.test.ts`). Mutation check (on `75289afa`): 13 of 15 mutants killed, each by its own test; the survivors were the factor (not binding at 270 s, above) and the background pass's shortened deadline, which this revision removes.
